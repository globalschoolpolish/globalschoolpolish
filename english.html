Introduction: Why I don't recommend this school to anyone?

The decision to take a language exam is usually highly stressful, a huge intellectual effort, and a significant financial expense. I expected professionalism, equal opportunities for all candidates, and transparency. Unfortunately, at the Global School Polish for Joy in Krakow, I encountered the exact opposite.

What happened during my exams (e.g., the TELC exam) resembled some kind of bad joke. Falsifying results of the oral part, favoring their own students, bribes in the form of "gifts," and absurd organizational conditions. Here is my full account of this nightmare.

Scandal #1: Gifts and "buying" the examiners' favor

The first thing that struck me at this facility was the blatant and shameless acceptance of "gifts." Right before I was about to take my oral exam, another male candidate appeared in the hallway. However, he didn't come empty-handed. He brought a large gift, the value of which could roughly be estimated at around 500 - 1000 PLN.

"Not only did the examiners not refuse to accept this gift (which should be an absolute standard in objective grading), but they eagerly accepted it right before the oral commission started."

How can we talk about objectivity when the commission accepts expensive gifts from a candidate right before grading them? This is blatant falsification of the oral part results and buying one's grade. The other candidates, who came simply to write and pass the exam based on their knowledge, are at a disadvantage right from the start.

Scandal #2: The course of the written exam and breaking procedures

Favoring "their own" students was visible at every step, also during the written exam. The examiners were noticeably nicer to them. They walked around the room, approached people they clearly knew from their paid courses, and said: "Oh, I know you!". They solicitously asked them if everything was alright. This creates enormous discomfort and stress for outsiders, who are treated like intruders.

The most important and absolutely unacceptable thing, however, was the breaking of basic rules for conducting state / certified exams.

  • No reaction to a phone call: During the exam, someone in the room didn't turn off their phone. Suddenly, an SMS arrived on the phone with a loud notification sound. At any normal exam, this would result in being asked to leave the room or the exam being terminated. Here? The examiners didn't stop it at all, they ignored the situation.
  • Breaks full of cronyism: During the break, the examiners straightforwardly asked their student examinees "how things were going", "how they did after the exam in April". This is an outright pathology.

Scandal #3: Fictitious registration for the C1 exam

I wanted to register for the C1 level exam at this school today (I still deluded myself that this might be a tolerable institution - my mistake). The application form was supposed to be available online exactly at 9:00 AM.

I had the page open for half an hour. I refreshed the site regularly every 5 minutes. The form was not there. After about an hour of waiting, a message suddenly popped up saying that... there are no more free places left! Even though the form itself was never available to me (and probably to the rest of the "ordinary" people) for even a moment.

The conclusion is simple and outrageous: they only register their own students for these exams, who pay them 5000 PLN for a course and buy them "gifts". Public registration is just a facade.

Scandal #4: Rigged oral exam and absurd logistics

The way people were paired up for the oral exam was another display of nepotism. During the examination commission, two best friends were paired together. I do not believe this was any kind of coincidence. They had attended courses with them before, so the examiners took care of them the whole time and arranged the most comfortable conditions for them.

And what was the commuting situation like? The written exam took place in some rented hotel in Krakow, in Nowa Huta. Right after it, we had to travel to the school's office for the oral commission.

  • They allowed an impossibly short time to get there.
  • People who had the commission at 11:45 simply couldn't physically make it there on time.
  • The commissions lasted from 11:45 to 16:00. Interestingly, the favored friends from the school had their commission ideally at 12:30 – they could commute calmly, didn't have to rush, and didn't have to wait long to return home.
  • I, as an "outsider," had my exam at 16:00. I was set as the absolutely last examinee on that day.

Scandal #5: Terrible office conditions (34 degrees and no chairs)

If you think that just making it to their office by 16:00 is the end of the trouble, you are dead wrong. The language school is located in some old tenement house.

On the day of the exam, the outside temperature was 34 degrees Celsius. No air conditioning whatsoever! The air conditioning only worked in the closed office, where the employees were sitting, but not in the hallway for the waiting candidates.

There was a terrible stuffiness in the hallway, there weren't even any windows to let in some fresh air. The worst part, however, was that for about 40 examinees, the school provided in the hallway... about 5 chairs!

"The result? Because of my exam at 16:00, I had to STAND in this unbearable stuffiness, without windows, in a temperature of over 30 degrees for 3 solid hours before an important oral exam!"

Summary

Global School Polish for Joy is the worst educational experience I have ever had to deal with. Lack of professionalism, breaking exam regulations, accepting suspicious packages ("gifts"), and terrible treatment of a customer who didn't leave 5000 PLN with them for a course.

If you care about a fair exam, objective grading, equal opportunities, and humane conditions in the room (air conditioning and at least a chair to sit on), avoid this school at all costs!
